# Resource policy

`RESOURCES.md` is the curated knowledge base for factual teaching. It should be small enough to remain useful.

## Stable format

```md
# Resources

## R001 — {title}
- Type: official documentation | standard | textbook | course | paper | community | ...
- Authority: {author/institution}
- URL: {url}
- Version/date: {when relevant}
- Use for: {specific branches/nodes}
- Limitations: {scope, outdated sections, bias, paywall, etc.}
```

## Trust order

The right hierarchy varies by domain, but prefer:
- primary standards/specifications and official documentation;
- professional bodies/guidelines;
- peer-reviewed primary or high-quality synthesis;
- respected university courses;
- canonical textbooks/references;
- recognized expert material;
- well-moderated practitioner communities for wisdom rather than foundational factual authority.

Marketing material, anonymous SEO content, and model-generated summaries are weak sources.

## Version sensitivity

For software, law, guidelines, standards, exams, or any changing domain:
- record version/date;
- re-check before teaching claims that may have changed;
- mark obsolete resources rather than silently continuing to use them.

## Curriculum sources vs teaching resources

`curriculum/SOURCES.md` records what justified the shape of the map.

`RESOURCES.md` records what is useful for learning/teaching.

A source may appear in both, but the purposes differ.

## Resource gaps

Keep a clear `## Gaps` section when important branches lack trustworthy material. A known gap is better than a fabricated source.

## Communities

Record credible communities separately when they are useful for judgment/feedback. Do not equate popularity with authority.
